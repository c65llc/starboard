screens.yaml

screens.yaml, in the content root (docs/flows/ by default), is the registry: every screen, its layout, and the screen files deliberately left out. Its JSON Schema is https://starboard.c65llc.com/schema/v1/screens.json (see JSON Schemas); a problem with it is reported as schema-invalid, or under a more specific rule named below.

From examples/desktop-notes:

Key Value
screens The screens, keyed by screen ID.
unmapped Files that match a platform’s screenFiles but are deliberately not screens, each with a path and a reason. A file listed here must not also be claimed by a screen (unmapped-and-claimed).

starboard fmt sorts screens by their area’s place in areas, then by ID, and writes each field in the order of the tables below.

Screens

Section titled “Screens”

A screen ID is two or more dot-separated parts, each of lowercase letters, digits and hyphens starting with a letter: trip.timeline, auth.sign-in (id-invalid, duplicate-screen).

Field Required Value
title yes The screen’s name for people.
area yes One of areas in starboard.yaml (unknown-area).
purpose no One sentence on what the screen is for (missing-purpose warns without it).
presentation yes root, push, tab, sheet, dialog, window or route (unknown-presentation).
status no shipped (the default) or proposed, or a mapping of platform to either. See Status.
platforms no The platforms the screen is on. Default: every configured platform (unknown-platform).
platformNote with a platforms subset Why the screen is not on every platform (platform-note-missing).
states no Names of the states the screen can be in, such as empty or read-only, for readers. Starboard does not check them.
files no Per platform, the source files that implement the screen: exact paths from the project root, not patterns. Each must exist unless the screen is proposed on that platform (file-missing). Two screens may claim the same file.
screenshots no Per platform, one screenshot: an exact path from the project root (screenshot-missing). The catalog shows it beside the wireframes.
layout no The default layout: a list of regions.
layouts no Layouts for particular form factors, keyed by form factor. Each replaces the whole default layout for that form factor (unknown-form-factor).

A screen whose presentation is sheet or dialog is drawn over a dimmed generic screen, and a window gets window chrome.

Regions

Section titled “Regions”

Each entry of layout, of a layouts override, of a container’s children and a list’s item is a region:

Field Value
region The region’s name: lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, starting with a letter. Required everywhere except on a list’s item. Unique within one layout tree, items included (duplicate-region).
kind Required. One of the kinds below (unknown-kind).
label What the content is, not its final copy. Without one, the wireframe shows the name. label-too-long warns past 40 characters.
to The ID of the screen it links to (a screen, never screen#region). It must be registered (edge-unresolved) and share a platform with this screen, unless this region has a platformNote (edge-platform-mismatch).
requires One name for what a person must be allowed to do to see or use the region. Checked against the requires vocabulary when there is one (vocabulary-unknown).
status shipped or proposed. A proposed region on a shipped screen proposes a change; a shipped region on a proposed screen is an error (status-conflict).
platformNote Why this region links to a screen on other platforms.
width For a child of a row: pixels (220) or a fraction of the row (1/3).
children Containers only: the regions inside, a list of regions.
item list only: the one region each row is drawn as.
empty list only: the empty state, {label: …}.
items tabs, nav and menu only: the entries.

Each entry of items has a label (required), and may have to, requires and status, which mean what they mean on a region.

Every to, including those in item and items, is a link in the app map.

A layouts override uses the same region names as the default layout for the same parts of the screen, so a flow’s @screen#region reference holds on every form factor. edge-parity warns when an override drops a link the default layout has. See Desktop and web layouts.

Kinds

Section titled “Kinds”

Each kind below shows a layout and the wireframe starboard render draws from it, in a small 360 × 320 frame. In a full screen, the regions of a layout stack top to bottom: a bar first in the layout pins to the top of the frame, tabs last pins to the bottom, and list, form and the containers share the height that is left. Region names appear in small monospace at the top right, a requires shows a lock and its name, and a proposed region is dashed. What does not fit is clipped, with a marker.

column

Section titled “column”

A container that stacks its children top to bottom.

row

Section titled “row”

A container that sets its children side by side. A child’s width is pixels or a fraction of the row; children without one share the rest equally.

section

Section titled “section”

A container that groups related regions under one name, stacked like a column.

bar

Section titled “bar”

A container for a toolbar or app bar: its children sit side by side. A bar first in a layout pins to the top of the frame.

tabs

Section titled “tabs”

A tab bar: its items side by side. tabs last in a layout pins to the bottom of the frame.

nav

Section titled “nav”

A sidebar: its items stacked top to bottom. On a wide layout it is usually the first child of a row.

menu

Section titled “menu”

A menu, or a set of choices such as a picker: its items in a row.

header

Section titled “header”

A heading or title.

text

Section titled “text”

A line or paragraph of text.

list

Section titled “list”

A list of rows. Its item is drawn three times; its empty label is the list’s empty state.

card

Section titled “card”

One card: a self-contained summary, often a list’s item.

media

Section titled “media”

An image or video, drawn as a crossed box.

map

Section titled “map”

A map, drawn as a crossed box taller than media.

banner

Section titled “banner”

A message across the screen, such as a warning or a notice.

form

Section titled “form”

A form, drawn as one box, when its fields need no names of their own. Use a section of fields when they do.

field

Section titled “field”

One input: a text field, a picker or a toggle.

action

Section titled “action”

A button or link.

primary-action

Section titled “primary-action”

The screen’s main action, drawn darker than an action.
